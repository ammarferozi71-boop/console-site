# META TITLE
Xbox Series X|S Storage: Expansion Card or USB Drive?

# META DESCRIPTION
Xbox says a USB drive stores any Xbox game but cannot play games Optimized for Series X|S; its Seagate expansion card can. What runs from where.

# ARTICLE BODY
**A USB drive can store any Xbox game, but it cannot play games Optimized for Xbox Series X|S; the Seagate Storage Expansion Card can, according to Xbox's own comparison of the two, published in 2020.** [e10] So the choice comes down to which games you want to launch without moving them first. [e10]

## What runs from where

| Game type | Internal SSD | Seagate Storage Expansion Card | USB 3.1 hard drive or SSD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Optimized for Xbox Series X\|S** | Plays | Plays | Stores only; does not play |
| **Xbox One games upgraded for Series X\|S** | Plays | Plays | Stores only; does not play |
| **Xbox One (not upgraded), Xbox 360 and original Xbox** | Plays | Plays | Plays |

Source for the first and third rows: Xbox Wire's storage primer, an official post published ahead of the November 2020 console launch. [e10] Windows Central's storage guide, last updated 17 May 2023, gives the same rule and is the source for the second row: Xbox One games upgraded for Series X|S are treated like Optimized games. [e5]

## What a USB drive is still good for

- **Playing older games.** Xbox One, Xbox 360 and original Xbox games play directly from a USB 3.1 drive. [e10] The exception is an Xbox One game that has been upgraded for Series X|S, which Windows Central says follows the Optimized rule. [e5]
- **Parking Optimized games.** Xbox says USB storage can hold Series X|S games for quick transfer back to the console when you want to play them, and that games can be moved or copied between internal and external storage. [e10]

The trade-off is speed. Xbox's table says a USB drive does not match the internal SSD, and the post says backward-compatible games load significantly faster from the internal SSD or the expansion card. [e10]

## What an expansion card adds

- **It plays Optimized games directly.** Xbox's table lists the Seagate card as playing Series X|S Optimized games and matching the internal SSD's speed and performance. [e10] Xbox's product page for the Seagate card makes the same speed claim: the same peak speed and performance as the console's internal SSD. [e11]
- **It works in both consoles.** Xbox says the card plugs into the Storage Expansion Port on either a Series X or a Series S with no setup. [e10]

These are Xbox's own statements; this site has not tested either card. [e10] [e11]

## Which cards and sizes are listed

| Card | Sizes listed | Where the list comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seagate Storage Expansion Card | 1 TB, 2 TB, 4 TB | Xbox's product page |
| WD_BLACK C50 Storage Expansion Card | 512 GB, 1 TB, 2 TB | Sandisk's product page, which describes the card as Xbox-licensed |

Sizes are as listed on those two pages when read on 11 October 2026. [e11] [e12] Neither page showed a price, and Xbox's page says prices and availability may vary by retailer, so this page gives none. [e11] [e12] These are the two cards we found maker or platform pages for, not a complete list.

Xbox's 2020 table covers the Seagate card only. [e10] For the WD_BLACK C50, Sandisk says the card uses the Xbox Expansion Slot and Xbox Velocity Architecture, emulates the console's internal SSD speeds and works with Quick Resume. [e12] The sources read do not include a statement from Xbox that the C50 plays Optimized games, so check the product listing for that before you buy one. [e10] [e12]

## Which should you buy?

- **You mostly play games Optimized for Series X|S and the internal drive is full:** the Seagate expansion card is the only add-on storage in Xbox's table that plays those games. [e10] For the WD_BLACK C50, see the note above.
- **You mostly play Xbox 360, original Xbox or Xbox One games that have not been upgraded for Series X|S:** a USB 3.1 drive plays them directly. [e10] [e5]
- **You want to keep Optimized games downloaded and do not mind moving one back before playing:** a USB drive works as storage for them. [e10]
- **You are still choosing a console:** see [Xbox Series X vs Series S](/xbox/xbox-series-x-vs-series-s).

## What this page does not cover

- **Prices.** Neither product page showed one. [e11] [e12] The only prices in the sources read are launch prices from 2020 and 2023, which may be out of date, so none are given here. [e10] [e5]
- **How long a transfer takes.** No source read gives a figure, and this site has measured none.
- **Xbox's current support article.** It could not be read for this page, so the what-runs-where rule rests on Xbox's 2020 post and the 2023 Windows Central guide. [e10] [e5] If a system update has changed the rule since, this page does not reflect it.
